# DECISIONS.md

One line per call: date, decision, and why. Newest at the bottom. Change a decision by adding a new line, not by editing an old one.

## Product

- 2026-09-15: Owned pieces are not always the anchor. Why: most people struggle to find art they love and don't know what they want.
- 2026-09-17: Walldrobe (then Walls on Rotation) is the class venture, replacing the food hall.
- 2026-09-21: Rotation is the product; the layout is the service that makes it fit.
- 2026-09-21: When a print is swapped, the old one goes back. Why: returns are the Rent the Runway model.
- 2026-09-21: Trade-in credit dropped, replaced by a small discount. Why: people would trade in what they like least.
- 2026-09-21: Renting originals from local artists is core, not a later add-on.
- 2026-09-30: Prices are set aside for the build. The product doesn't charge in v1; buy links show each source's own price.
- 2026-09-21: Name is Walldrobe, a wardrobe for your walls. "Rent the Runway, for art" stays as a small line. Usage: "pick out something new from your Walldrobe." Rejected: Composed, Walls on Rotation, Reframe.
- 2026-09-30: v1 buys by linking out to each piece's product page. Why: shipping was always the hardest part.
- 2026-09-30: Start with one wall from one photo. Whole apartment later.
- 2026-09-30: Keep flags. Must keep: always on this wall, but the engine may move it. Happy to move: the engine may use it or leave it off this wall. Don't care: used only if it helps. A separate "pin where it is" toggle for a piece that can't move. Rejected: must keep means it stays exactly where it hangs.
- 2026-09-30: Demo art only from CC0 open-access collections (Art Institute of Chicago, The Met, National Gallery of Art, Rijksmuseum). Buyable pieces are link-outs; their images aren't re-hosted unless the source's feed allows it. Local artists only with their OK; anything else labeled as an example.

## Engineering

- 2026-09-30: Rules and scoring for layout and geometry; models only for taste and image understanding. Why: the Dreamy lesson, where an LLM writing plans got expensive and unreliable.
- 2026-09-30: Scanner is web first: one photo, four corner taps, one known measurement, perspective correction in the browser. A native iPhone scanner (RoomPlan with LiDAR) comes later and feeds the same engine. Rejected: native first. Why: no portfolio embed, and slower to test with real people.
- 2026-09-30: The engine is plain JavaScript ES modules with no dependencies, running unchanged in the browser and in Node, tested with node:test. Why: the app will be single-file Preact plus htm with no build step, and `?demo` has to work with no backend. Reopen: if scoring needs heavy math.
- 2026-09-30: Inches internally, origin at the floor on the left edge of the wall, y up. Why: that's how people measure.
- 2026-09-30: Hanging rules v1: centerline 57 in (57 to 60 on a bare wall); gaps 2.5 in (2 to 3); group about 2/3 the width of the furniture below; bottom edge 8 in above a couch back or headboard (6 to 10); over furniture, clearance wins over the centerline. Reopen: after five real walls.
- 2026-09-30: New pieces come in a fixed set of standard frame sizes. Why: the frames stay on the wall, so a swapped print has to fit the same frame.
- 2026-09-30: Scoring weights v1: fit 0.35, taste 0.30, harmony 0.20, balance 0.15. Guesses until the tuning pipeline or real walls say otherwise.
- 2026-09-30: Fit also scores presence (a thin strip over a couch loses to a group with height) and keeps must-keep pieces near the middle. Balance also marks down layouts heavy at the top. Why: the first elevations showed a row of small prints winning over the couch and a must-keep piece drifting to the edge.
- 2026-09-30: Owned pieces get an edge over new art: +0.15 in the pick for happy to move, +0.05 for don't care, and up to +0.04 on the final score. Why: using what you already own is the premise; without it a new print with the same taste score always won.
- 2026-09-30: Unknown obstacle kinds (a lamp, an aquarium) are treated as blockers. Why: the safe default is to keep art off anything we don't understand.
- 2026-09-30: After the first code review: must-keeps always take a statement center; a group over furniture may slide at most 15% of its width off center; positions round to the quarter inch before the hard checks; widths are generated only inside 0.5 to 0.8 of the furniture (0.45 to 0.7 of a bare wall); over tall furniture the center rises as needed and is marked down above 66 in; the search keeps the 40 best-liked pieces per frame size. Why: sixteen findings from a fresh review, each reproduced.
- 2026-09-30: Later, an offline pipeline where a cheaper model tags catalog art and reviews sample layouts to tune weights. Never at request time.
- 2026-09-30: Public repo, jason-traum/walldrobe, docs included. Why: it's a portfolio project for jason-traum.github.io.
- 2026-09-30: Security: every database write goes through a function that checks who is asking; only the publishable key in the frontend; wall photos private by default.

- 2026-09-30: Demo art is modern photography and painting from Unsplash (Unsplash License: free to show with credit, not sold), picked for what people actually hang: aerial pools and beaches, Mediterranean coast, tennis courts, Palm Springs, black-and-white film, neutral abstracts, food and drink. Credited to each photographer with a link. Replaces the CC0 museum line above for the demo. Why: Jason wants cool modern art and photos, not museum pieces; modern art is under copyright, and Unsplash is free to show. Unsplash+ premium photos are excluded.
- 2026-09-30: Taste v0 is a Bradley-Terry model over simple features (category, painting or photo, light, vivid, warm or cool, black and white), fit in the browser from 7 fixed quiz pairs. The demo opens on a labeled sample taste. Reopen: when image embeddings are precomputed for the catalog.
- 2026-09-30: Catalog grows to 275 pieces across 32 categories, with no more than about ten of one subject. Added cars, western, horses, dogs, sailing, ski, golf, coffee, drinks, food, sculpture, objects, city, water, sky, moon, shadows, graphic and beach. Why: Jason asked for at least 200, picked for what people actually hang.
- 2026-09-30: Every piece is one record in the CATALOG.md shape, checked by engine/catalog.js on every build and test. Fields say who set them (source, measured, rule, model, human). Color and composition are measured by tools/analyze.py; tags start as rules and are meant to be replaced by the tagging pipeline or a person.
- 2026-09-30: Taste v1 learns from the record: theme, style and mood tags plus measured light, contrast, color, warmth, busyness and empty space. The taste test is 10 picks; skips don't count. The first four pairs contrast themes, then it alternates between testing what it leans toward and exploring what it doesn't know. It says what it learned in plain words. Why: v0 used categories only and asked where it was least sure, which kept showing pairs where both pieces matched.
- 2026-09-30: Themes are summer, sport, city, nature, still life, art, animals and mono. Sport split out of summer so ski photos aren't filed as art or summer.
- 2026-09-30: Engine v2 judges the whole wall. Scoring weights v2: fit 0.25, taste 0.25, color 0.25, design 0.25, plus the reuse bonus; replaces the v1 weights above. Color checks the harmony scheme, 60/30/10 proportions, repeated accents, warm against cool, saturation, value and the room. Design checks balance, a focal piece, rhythm, variety, side pieces facing in and mirrored weight. Why: Jason asked for real color theory and design concepts; v1 judged pieces one at a time. Weights are guesses until real walls or the tuning pipeline say otherwise.
- 2026-09-30: Every catalog piece records how much of it is each of 12 color families, a 12-slice color wheel weighted by how colorful each pixel is, how much of it is colorful at all, and its dark, mid and light split. Owned pieces and the room get an estimate from their palette.
- 2026-09-30: Schemes are judged on the painter's color wheel (red, orange, yellow, green, blue, violet), mapped from Lab hue by fixed anchors. Why: in Lab, blue sits 120 degrees from orange, so blue and orange came out as "analogous".
- 2026-09-30: After the beam search, the best layouts get an improvement pass: the 12 best alternatives are tried in each open slot and kept when the whole-wall score goes up, two passes. The three layouts shown use different new art when the catalog has enough.
- 2026-09-30: Keep, swap, refresh and try another. Keep puts a catalog piece on every layout; swap changes one piece; refresh changes everything not kept and not yours, in the same frames and places; try another skips arrangements already shown, and says so when it has shown them all. Pieces you own always stay on a refresh. Why: Jason asked for options to keep pieces and refresh others or try a new layout.
- 2026-09-30: No near-duplicates on one wall. A look-alike score compares subject, colors, light, busyness, empty space and style for every pair; a pair at 0.88 or more (two moon photos, two near-identical pools) costs 0.04 off the total and gets a caveat, and the search steers away from them. Design weights become balance 0.25, focal 0.15, rhythm 0.15, variety 0.15, flow 0.1, mirror 0.05, distinct 0.15. Why: Jason: "we don't want to have three posters of water that look almost the exact same." A set should share a thread, not repeat itself.
- 2026-09-30: A size lever: fewer, bigger pieces, balanced, or more, smaller ones. Balanced leaves the choice to the other scores. Why: Jason asked for a lever for more and smaller or bigger and fewer pieces.
- 2026-09-30: The public page is the real Walldrobe site, not a demo (feedback: "I want to click into it like it's the app"). - 2026-10-01: The photo reader reads only the wall. A photo goes to its corners first: we suggest them (from the straight edges where the wall's color stops: the ceiling or a soffit, the side walls, and the floor, the lowest line the wall reaches), the person checks or drags them, and only the wall inside them is flattened and read. The floor is the bottom corners. A TV is split from its stand (a screen is about 16:9 and its top edge is against the wall) and sets the width only when it's clear of everything else; then a 55 in set, with the size there to change (65 in or 75 in only if 55 would make a ceiling the photo shows lower than 7 ft). A TV on a stand is taken as about a foot out from the wall, so it looks bigger than it would on it, and the wall is scaled up to match. When the floor is hidden (behind a table, say) and a TV stands on furniture, the bottom corners go 21 in below the TV's bottom (stands are mostly 20 to 24 in tall), and what stands in the hidden band runs down to that floor. With no such TV, the person gives one measurement; no quiet 10 ft default. A lamp's box takes in its shade. A vase or plant standing on furniture isn't marked. Why: Jason's living room photo (a 55 in TV) read the ceiling as the wall, found no art, four fake lamps, the table as the floor and a 120 in wall; the first fix still put the floor at the table's edge and didn't allow for the TV standing out, so it came out 6 ft tall. The same photo is now a test fixture (test/fixtures/living_room.png, 450 px wide so nothing on the table can be read).
- 2026-10-01: The photo reader uses an image model: SegFormer B2 trained on ADE20K (about 29 MB, quantized), run on the person's phone with onnxruntime-web; the photo never leaves it, only the model downloads, once. It labels each patch of the photo (wall, ceiling, floor, painting, TV, lamp, sofa, bed, window, door...). The labels decide what things are; the straight-line fitting decides exactly where the edges are; the earlier reader stays as the fallback when the model can't load. NVIDIA's license for these weights is non-commercial: fine for the free beta, and they get replaced (a model with a commercial license, or one trained on our own photos) before Walldrobe charges anyone. Why: Jason: "pick a better algo"; the color rules missed walls and art on most of his other photos.

Why: every House of Spoils print is framed at sizes like 18.5 x 14.5 and 26 x 19.5 in, which the fixed list never used, so in Prints mode most layouts were built from a handful of Desenio 12 x 16 prints.
- 2026-10-01: For art already hanging in the photo, "Must keep" is "Keep it there": it stays where it hangs and the new art goes around it. Why: Jason's test with the TV on and both prints set to must keep: no scale, and a dead end.
- 2026-10-01: Beside the TV, not only over it. On a wall with a TV or furniture the engine also tries the open stretch at eye level on each side of it (20 in wide or more), where the art fills more of the stretch (0.75 of it, 0.5 to 0.95) and may move up to 6 in up or down to clear furniture; a tall stack there may come down to 20 in from the floor. A new structured shape, a stack (two to four pieces, one over the other on one center line), is built only in such a narrow stretch or on open wall under 48 in. A "Where" choice (Anywhere, Over the TV, Left side, Right side) shows when there's more than one place. With Anywhere, the best layout that uses the most of your own happy-to-move pieces comes first, then over the TV, and at most one tab beside it. Why: Jason: "it's not giving me any options with art to the left like where I had it". His 34 in print didn't fit the 26 in over the TV, so both his prints were left off and nothing tried the wall where they hang now.
- 2026-10-01: Free-form layouts over all the open wall, and the "Where" choice is gone. The engine maps the open wall (minus buffers), finds starting spots where a mid-size frame fits (several per big pocket, nearest eye level) and over the TV or furniture, and grows layouts frame by frame, each next to one already up, one gap apart and lined up. It also builds two groups that answer each other across the TV or a window (the second starts on the first one's top or bottom line, at least 8 in away), keeps the person's pieces "as they are" as an option, and repairs the best shapes (drop a piece, shift a group, move the least attached piece, swap a size). Shapes are judged as a picture: fullness against a target, art against the open area inside each group's outline (so wrapping a TV isn't punished), a few strong shared lines, no pieces hanging off by a corner, eye level, relation to the furniture, and balance across the whole wall. Ranking is staged: fit and design make a composition score; layouts more than 0.15 below the best are dropped when enough remain; then taste and color rank the rest (composition 0.5, taste 0.25, color 0.25), and using their own pieces is worth twice what it was. The first layout uses the most of their pieces; then a one-group free-form, a two-group one, a set shape, and "as it is". Why: Jason ("look at all avail space ... like a human brain would", "it shouldn't say above the TV or to the left") and the outside review by Astra (maximize-art pushes toward crowding; judge relationships; staged selection; repair).
- 2026-10-01: How full: Calm, Balanced (default) and Full set the target share of the open wall covered by art (0.15, 0.28, 0.45), with penalties for too little and too much. A lead piece is only expected when the sizes differ a lot; an equal set is fine.
- 2026-10-01: New prints come in a core of 7 shop sizes that nest (12x16, 14.5x18.5, 19.5x26, 20x28, 24.5x33.5, 31.5x44, 39.5x56, either way up) when the catalog offers at least 3 of them. Owned art stays at its real size. Why: fewer sizes that add up to each other line up better (two 14.5 in frames plus a 2.5 in gap make 31.5 in).
- 2026-10-01: "Must keep" is split. A piece already hanging can be "Stays put" (exactly where it is), "Must use" (in every layout, free to move), "Happy to move" or "Don't care". The piece count includes pieces that stay put. One more or one fewer keeps the frames already up where they are. Art that isn't up yet can be added by size, with an optional photo of it for its colors and shape.
- 2026-10-01: Photo reader, from a run on 81 stock room photos: a screen that's off and evenly black inside (dark, little texture, about 16:9) is the TV even when the image model calls it a painting, so wall-mounted TVs are found; a dark rectangle the model labels as a sofa, armchair, bed, cushion or chair is never the TV (couch backs were being read as TVs and setting the wall's size); and the edge of a frame, TV, window, lamp, plant or furniture is never taken as the wall's own side or top edge.
- 2026-10-01: Things read from a photo get 1.5 in more clearance (`fuzz`), for the error in reading their edges. Once a person moves or retypes a box, the extra room goes.
- 2026-10-01: Pieces can be moved by hand on the layouts screen ("Move pieces"), before the nail step, as agreed. Snapping to other frames, the layout's spacing, the wall's middle, 57 in and the edges of the TV and furniture; a spot that breaks a hard rule (clearances, the minimum gap, the wall's ends) is refused and the piece goes back. Pieces that stay put don't move. A moved layout keeps its spots through refresh, swap and count steps, and is the one kept on reload.
- 2026-10-01: The hanging guide takes the wire drop (default 2 in) and every nail height follows it. When the wall's size came from the photo, nail spots are labeled estimates with a link to check one measurement; typing the width on the check screen counts as a measurement. Each spot is also given from the nearest hard edge within 30 in (TV, window, door, mirror, shelf, dresser, console), not couches or beds, whose edges are soft.

- 2026-10-01: The wall's scale comes from every reference in the photo at once, not the TV alone: the TV (any size from 43 to 75 in, so loosely), a door at 6 ft 8 in (tightly), a queen bed, a couch at about 7 ft, an 8 ft ceiling when asked for, and an outlet plate at 4.5 in tall (tight, but weak when small in the photo). They average by confidence; one more than 25% off the others is left out when two others agree; when the rest disagree with the TV by more than 15% its size is re-picked from the list to agree with them (unless the person chose it); and when what counts still spans more than 15% the confirm screen says both widths and asks for a measurement. A typed width is the whole answer. With no TV clear of everything else the person still measures, as before. Why: one assumed TV size set every wall, and a door or a couch in the same photo could have said it was wrong.
