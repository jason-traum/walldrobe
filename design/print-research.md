# Where to print: research for Walldrobe (US, checked 2026-10-03)

Why: Walldrobe suggests free photos (Unsplash, Pexels, Pixabay) at standard frame sizes. The person needs somewhere to print each one at the right size. This compares print services on price per size, pickup and shipping, discounts, quality and whether an app could send the order directly. Three research passes, each on the services' own pages (and their own product data where prices load by script); deal sites only for sale history. Prices are regular list prices, before codes and shipping. "n/o" means the size isn't offered; "nv" means not verified.

## The short answer

| Use | Pick | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Cheapest everyday | Walmart Photo | Lowest regular price at every size it offers, Fuji Crystal Archive paper, same-day pickup for matte posters up to 20x30, free shipping over $35 |
| Same day, large | CVS or Walgreens | Same-day posters up to 24x36; weekly codes of 40 to 70% off; CVS has the only paper 18x24 among the retailers |
| Nicer prints, mailed | Mpix or Printique | Pro labs; Printique is cheaper on paper, Mpix ships free over $45 and has a live 10% code |
| An app sending the order itself | Social Print Studio's PrintKit | Public API, no key: send a size and a photo URL, get back a checkout link. But see the license note |

## License note (read before any revenue share)

Pexels says plainly: "Don't sell unaltered copies of a photo or video, e.g. as a poster, print or on a physical product without modifying it first." Unsplash and Pixabay have similar rules on selling unaltered copies as prints (check each license before relying on this). A person printing a free photo for their own wall is fine. Walldrobe taking money on that print (a revenue share, an affiliate cut, or selling it through a print API) starts to look like selling the photo as a print, and CLAUDE.md already says the free art is "never sold". So for free photos: link out, take nothing. Affiliate links are fine for shop prints and for frames.

## Quality

- Retail (Walmart, CVS, Walgreens, Snapfish, Shutterfly): fine for posters; reviewers note darker or slightly green prints. Snapfish and Shutterfly auto-enhance by default; turn it off.
- Pro labs: Printique topped the newest review (PetaPixel, Jan 2026; affiliate disclosure). Mpix "Best Value", strong color. Bay Photo is the most pro-grade (prints darker; test first). Nations rated average to good.
- No recent independent blind test covers all of them.

## For a future integration

- Social Print Studio PrintKit (printkit.dev): no key; POST a size and a public photo URL, get a checkout URL; 20 to 30% revenue share. Easiest by far, but the revenue share is the license problem above.
- Walgreens Photo Prints API: docs live; upload, prices, store search, same-day orders; needs a key, affiliate ID and a signed agreement; whether new partners are approved in 2026 is nv.
- Bay Photo: partner API with approval; public deep links preselect a product (no photo).
- Printful, Lumaprints (sandbox), Artelo, FinerWorks (test mode), Gooten: business APIs where Walldrobe pays and charges the customer, which is selling prints.
- Everyone else: product deep links only, no photo attached.
